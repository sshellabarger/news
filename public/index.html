Eaton picks Dogtown: a $242 million plant and 1,200 jobs

Power-management giant Eaton announced Wednesday it will build a $242 million, million-square-foot manufacturing facility in North Little Rock — roughly 1,200 jobs making customized electrical enclosures for its Fibrebond business, feeding the data-center, utility, and industrial boom. The company says the plant will double its U.S. enclosure capacity; Mayor Terry Hartwick called the project a major economic win for the whole central Arkansas region. The deal was worked by the Arkansas Economic Development Commission, the city, and the Metro Little Rock Alliance.

Police · The surveillance files · Filed Aug 23

Four million images a month, and no public accounting for the stops

North Little Rock is doubling down on license plate readers — 20 more Flock Safety cameras bought this spring for $70,000, which will bring the city's Flock total to 32 once installed. But the Democrat-Gazette reports the police department has published no data this year on the traffic stops those cameras produce, despite a 2025 state law that requires agencies to compile the results every six months for public consumption. In one 30-day window, the department's own IT division found, the cameras captured roughly four million images. Statewide, Greers Ferry, Mayflower, Cabot, and Pea Ridge have all dropped their Flock readers this month.

“The revised policy will include regular audits of LPR usage.”— Mayor Terry Hartwick, who says the department's policy is being rewritten

Police · The overtime files

Unworked hours, unbilled companies: off-duty police overtime cost taxpayers $500,000

A report by then-CFO Ember Strange found the city paid for off-duty overtime hours that were never worked and failed to bill companies accurately for officers' side work — roughly $1.2 million logged for private businesses in a year. In one December pay period, nine officers took home more than the mayor. The city has hired accounting firm Forvis Mazars to audit the department's timekeeping and GPS records, and the department's top internal-affairs officer is among those whose pay drew scrutiny. “There's no telling how much money we've paid overall over the past few years for people not working!!!” city attorney Amy Fields wrote when the report landed.

About · Dogtown News

North Little Rock's neighborhood wire

Dirty Dogtown is North Little Rock, Arkansas' independent neighborhood news site — Dogtown news swept from public sources every hour and tipped by neighbors. We watch city hall, the police blotter, the parks, and the schools for all of North Little Rock: Argenta, Park Hill, Levy, Rose City, Baring Cross, Lakewood, Amboy, and beyond.

Why is North Little Rock called Dogtown?

As the story goes, a century back Little Rock sent its stray dogs across the river, and the north shore kept the name out of spite — then wore it with pride. Dirty Dogtown carries the tradition: North Little Rock's news, unsigned and unbossed.

How often is Dirty Dogtown updated?

The wire sweeps public sources for North Little Rock news every hour, the front page renumbers daily, and subscribers get a morning email digest. Stories older than about two weeks rest in The Morgue, our searchable archive.

How do I send in North Little Rock news tips?

Use the Send a tip form — no account and no name required. A moderator reviews every submission before anything runs.
