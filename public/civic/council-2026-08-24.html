Threads we're watching

Ongoing city hall stories from our coverage — not necessarily on tonight's agenda, but the room where they move.

The plate readers and the missing data

The city is expanding to 32 Flock cameras while NLRPD has published none of the traffic-stop data a 2025 state law requires every six months. Mayor Hartwick says the policy is being rewritten to include regular audits — the council is where that accountability lands.

The old central fire station's second life

In July the council cleared Loblolly Creamery to negotiate a possible $825,000 purchase of the former central fire station at 723 Maple St. Any deal would come back through this room.

The police overtime audit

Forvis Mazars is auditing NLRPD timekeeping after a report found the city paid for off-duty overtime hours never worked and misbilled companies for officers' side work. Findings and any fixes land in front of the council.

After the Dickey-Stephens vote

The 20-year Travelers lease passed 5–2 on Aug. 10. Implementation details — and whatever the two no votes were worried about — are worth tracking.

Four-way race for Ward 2

Linda Robinson's open seat draws four candidates this November. Council meetings between now and then double as auditions.
